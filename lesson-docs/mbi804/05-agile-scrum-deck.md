## 1. Purpose & learning objectives

## 2. Full content

The deck renders each slide from a hardcoded HTML string at fixed 1920×1080 "design pixels," scaled to fit the viewer. All 22 slides, condensed to their substantive content (colors/decorative elements omitted, factual content preserved in full):

1. **Title** — "Agile Scrum Process in IT" / "A complete visual guide to Scrum roles, artifacts, events, and the Sprint cycle as used in real-world IT software delivery." Badges: Roles & Teams · Artifacts · Ceremonies · Sprint Cycle.
2. **What is Agile?** — Definition: "Agile is an iterative approach to software development and project management that helps teams deliver value to customers faster and with fewer headaches." Key characteristics: short delivery cycles, continuous stakeholder feedback, self-organising cross-functional teams, embracing change even late, working software as the primary measure of progress. Origin note: the Agile Manifesto was written in 2001 by 17 software practitioners in Snowbird, Utah.
3. **Agile Manifesto — Four Core Values** — the four canonical value pairs ("we value X over Y"): Individuals & Interactions over Processes & Tools; Working Software over Comprehensive Documentation; Customer Collaboration over Contract Negotiation; Responding to Change over Following a Plan. Closing quote: "while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more."
4. **Agile vs. Waterfall** — a comparison table across 7 rows: delivery approach (single end delivery vs. incremental every sprint), requirements (fixed upfront/costly changes vs. evolving/welcomed anytime), customer involvement (start & end only vs. continuous), testing phase (after development complete vs. continuous every sprint), team structure (siloed Dev/QA/BA vs. cross-functional self-organising), risk management (discovered late/expensive vs. surfaced early/often), best suited for (fixed-scope stable requirements vs. complex evolving software).
5. **What is Scrum?** — Definition: "Scrum is a lightweight Agile framework for developing, delivering, and sustaining complex products — most commonly used in software development." Sprint duration 1–4 weeks, team size 3–9 members. Three Pillars of Scrum: Transparency ("all significant aspects of the process must be visible to everyone responsible for the outcome"), Inspection ("Scrum users must frequently inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal to detect undesirable variances"), Adaptation ("if inspection reveals deviation beyond acceptable limits, the process must be adjusted as soon as possible").
6. **Scrum Framework at a Glance** — a horizontal flow diagram: Product Backlog → Sprint Planning → [the Sprint, 1–4 weeks: Sprint Backlog → Dev Work, with a Daily Scrum · 15 min/day badge] → Sprint Review + Retrospective → Increment (shippable product value).
7. **Three Scrum Roles overview** — three role cards: Product Owner ("maximises product value" — owns the Product Backlog, prioritises by business value, defines acceptance criteria, voice of customer/stakeholders, accepts/rejects sprint outcomes; accountability: maximising product value); Scrum Master ("servant-leader & coach" — facilitates all Scrum events, removes impediments, coaches on Scrum practices, shields the team from outside interruptions, promotes continuous improvement; accountability: ensuring Scrum is understood and enacted); Developers ("cross-functional delivery team" — build the increment each sprint, self-organise, collectively own code quality, estimate effort in story points, define/uphold the Definition of Done; accountability: creating a usable Increment every Sprint meeting the DoD).
8. **Product Owner deep dive** — one person (not a committee) who owns and prioritises the Product Backlog. Core responsibilities (4 numbered steps): develop/communicate the Product Goal; create and refine Product Backlog items (writing/clarifying user stories); prioritise the backlog to maximise value per sprint; accept or reject sprint deliverables at Sprint Review. Key insight: the PO is the bridge between business and dev team, and their decisions directly determine ROI. PO interacts with: Stakeholders & Customers (gather requirements/priorities), Developers (clarify stories, answer questions), Scrum Master (coaching). Common mistake flagged: having multiple people act as PO — it must be one person with final authority.
9. **Scrum Master deep dive** — a servant-leader serving the Scrum Team, PO, and organisation. Three sub-roles: Educator (teaches Scrum to everyone), Remover (clears impediments/blockers), Facilitator (runs all Scrum events). Detail on serving three groups: Serving the PO (help manage/refine the backlog, facilitate stakeholder collaboration, ensure the PO knows how to maximise value); Serving the Developers (coach self-management/cross-functionality, remove external impediments, protect from interruptions); Serving the Organisation (lead/train/coach Scrum adoption, plan/advise on implementations, help employees understand empirical thinking).
10. **Developers deep dive** — 3–9 members, cross-functional by design. Team characteristics: cross-functional (collectively hold all skills needed), self-organising (team decides how to do the work), accountable as a team (no sub-teams/hierarchies within), committed to the Sprint Goal each iteration. Definition of Done callout: developers create and enforce a shared agreement on "done"; no increment ships without meeting it. Typical roles within: Software Engineers (features/APIs/databases), UI/UX Designers, QA Engineers, DevOps/Infrastructure (deployments, CI/CD). Ideal team size 3–9: too small lacks skills, too large explodes communication overhead.
11. **Three Scrum Artifacts overview** — Product Backlog ("the single, ordered list of everything that might be needed in the product," managed by the PO; its commitment is the Product Goal, "the long-term objective for the Scrum Team"), Sprint Backlog ("the Sprint Goal, the Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint, plus the plan for delivering the Increment," updated daily by Developers; its commitment is the Sprint Goal, "the single objective for the Sprint"), Increment ("a concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal," must be usable and meet the Definition of Done, multiple increments may exist within one Sprint; its commitment is the Definition of Done, "quality standard for every increment").
12. **Product Backlog deep dive** — "An ordered list of everything that needs to be done to improve the product. It is the single source of work for the Scrum Team. Never complete — always evolving." Example backlog table (5 rows): user registration (Critical, 5 pts), login+dashboard (Critical, 8 pts), profile photo upload (High, 3 pts), admin view all accounts (High, 5 pts), signup email notification (Medium, 3 pts). Key properties: ordered by value/risk/priority, refined continuously (Backlog Refinement), estimated in story points, transparent to all stakeholders. Backlog Refinement callout: the ongoing process of breaking down/detailing/estimating items, recommended ~10% of team capacity per sprint. Product Goal callout: the long-term commitment embedded in the backlog giving direction across multiple sprints.
13. **Sprint Backlog & Increment** — Sprint Backlog: a subset of the Product Backlog selected for the current Sprint plus the plan for achieving the Sprint Goal; belongs to the Developers — only they can change it mid-sprint. Contains: Sprint Goal (the "why"), selected PBIs, Tasks (breakdown of work), a daily plan updated at Daily Scrum. Increment: the sum of all completed items in a Sprint plus previous increments; must be usable and meet the DoD even if the PO decides not to release it. Example Definition of Done checklist (6 items): all code peer-reviewed; unit tests written and passing (>80% coverage); feature tested in staging; accessibility standards met (WCAG 2.1 AA); documentation updated (API docs/README); Product Owner has accepted the story.
14. **Five Scrum Events overview** — 5 timeboxed event cards: The Sprint (1–4 weeks, "the container for all other events"); Sprint Planning (max 8 hrs, "Why/What/How"); Daily Scrum (15 min/day); Sprint Review (max 4 hrs, "the team presents the Increment to stakeholders"); Sprint Retrospective (max 3 hrs, "the Scrum Team inspects how they worked"). Footnote: all timeboxes shown are for a 4-week Sprint — scale proportionally for shorter sprints (e.g. a 2-week sprint → Sprint Planning max 4 hours).
15. **The Sprint** — "a fixed-length event of one month or less. A new Sprint starts immediately after the conclusion of the previous one — no gaps." Duration 1–4 weeks (consistent length); contains all Scrum events; no changes endangering the Sprint Goal (though scope may be clarified/renegotiated with the PO); can be cancelled by the PO if the Goal becomes obsolete. "Why short sprints?" callout: frequent checkpoints reduce risk — wrong-direction discovery happens in 1–2 weeks, not 6 months. A worked 2-week sprint timeline: Day 1 Sprint Planning (set goal, select PBIs, plan tasks, max 4 hrs); Days 2–9 development + daily 15-min standups; Day 10 Sprint Review (demo to stakeholders, max 2 hrs); Day 10 Sprint Retrospective (max 1.5 hrs).
16. **Sprint Planning** — attended by the entire Scrum Team, max 8 hours for a 4-week Sprint. Three topics: Topic 1 — WHY is this Sprint valuable? (PO proposes how the sprint increases value; team collaboratively defines the Sprint Goal). Topic 2 — WHAT can be Done this Sprint? (Developers select PBIs they're confident completing — only they can judge their own capacity). Topic 3 — HOW will the work get done? (Developers decompose selected items into tasks, often under 1 day each, creating the Sprint Backlog). Output: a Sprint Backlog with the Goal, selected PBIs, and a task plan. Story Point Estimation: teams use Fibonacci-like points (1,2,3,5,8,13,21…) as relative effort ("8" ≈ 8× harder than "1"); a scale legend: 1–3 Small, 5–8 Medium, 13+ Large → Split! Velocity callout: total story points completed per sprint, used to forecast future capacity.
17. **Daily Scrum (Standup)** — 15 minutes, every day, same time & place, for Developers only. Purpose: inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary. Three classic questions (numbered): (1) What did I do yesterday that helped the team meet the Sprint Goal? (2) What will I do today to help the team meet the Sprint Goal? (3) Is there any impediment blocking me or the team from reaching the Sprint Goal? Key rules: strictly 15 minutes; Developers only (Scrum Master attends if needed, PO optional); same time/place daily; not a status report to management; impediments are noted and solved offline afterward. Anti-pattern flagged: turning the standup into a problem-solving session. Impact callout: eliminates the need for other meetings, keeps the team aligned, surfaces blockers early, creates ownership of the Sprint Goal.
18. **Sprint Review** — end of Sprint, max 4 hours, entire Scrum Team + Stakeholders. "The Scrum Team presents the results of their work to stakeholders and discusses progress toward the Product Goal. The Product Backlog may be adjusted based on feedback." What happens (4 steps): PO explains what was planned vs. completed; Developers demo the working increment live; stakeholders provide feedback; PO updates the Product Backlog based on discussion. Who attends: PO (presents & accepts work), Developers (demo live), Scrum Master (facilitates), Stakeholders (customers, managers, end users). Key difference flagged: Sprint Review is NOT a sign-off meeting — it's a collaborative working session. Output: a revised Product Backlog with probable next-Sprint items.
19. **Sprint Retrospective** — end of Sprint, max 3 hours, Scrum Team only (no external stakeholders). "The team inspects how they worked — not what they built." Three classic questions: What went well this Sprint? (keep doing); What did not go well? (stop/change); What improvements will we commit to next Sprint? What is inspected: Individuals (team dynamics/communication), Interactions (how the team collaborates), Processes (workflow/ceremonies/practices), Tools (Jira, Confluence, CI/CD), Definition of Done (is it still appropriate?). Output: the most impactful improvement items are added to the next Sprint Backlog. Safe-space callout: "What's said in the Retro stays in the Retro. Psychological safety is essential."
20. **User Stories & Acceptance Criteria** — Definition: "a short, plain-language description of a feature from the perspective of the end user... who wants something, what they want, and why." Standard format template: "As a [type of user] / I want [to perform some action] / So that [I can achieve some goal]." Two worked examples: "As a student, I want to view my attendance record, so that I know if I am at risk of failing due to absences." / "As a lecturer, I want to export attendance to CSV, so that I can share it with administration quickly." Acceptance Criteria definition: conditions a story must meet to be "Done," written by the PO, verified by the team. Worked AC example for "view attendance record" (5 items): student sees attendance % per subject; absence dates shown chronologically; a warning appears if attendance <80%; page loads under 2 seconds; works on mobile and desktop. INVEST criteria: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable.
21. **Scrum Board (Kanban)** — "A physical or digital board (e.g. Jira, Trello) tracking Sprint Backlog items across workflow stages. Updated daily at the Daily Scrum." A 4-column example board: To Do (user login page, password reset flow, email notifications, profile photo upload), In Progress (user registration form — Sarah; dashboard UI layout — James), In Review/QA (API authentication endpoint — Priya reviewing; database schema migration — Mark reviewing), Done (project scaffolding & CI setup, database design & ERD, tech stack decision, Sprint 0 planning complete). Popular tools listed: Jira, Trello, Linear, GitHub Projects, Azure DevOps, or a physical whiteboard with sticky notes.
22. **Scrum in the IT Industry** — Where Scrum is used: Web & Mobile App Development (React, iOS, Android), Cloud & SaaS Platforms (AWS, Azure product teams), AI/Data Science Projects (model development cycles), Cybersecurity & Networking (vulnerability management), Enterprise IT (SAP/ERP/CRM implementations). Business benefits (4 cards): Faster Delivery (value shipped every 1–4 weeks), Lower Risk (problems caught early/often), Better Collaboration (daily communication/alignment), Continuous Improvement (retrospectives drive growth). Closing stat: "87% of Agile teams use Scrum" — Source: 17th State of Agile Report.

## 3. UI & interaction design

Each slide is a fixed 1920×1080px `<section>` scaled to fit its container via a `ResizeObserver` that computes `scale = containerWidth / 1920` and applies a CSS transform — so the deck always renders at native "design pixel" proportions regardless of viewport, like a scaled-down presentation slide rather than a responsive page. A large injected `DECK_CSS` string (scoped under a `.scrum` class prefix) defines CSS custom properties for a green/navy palette (`--green:#059669`, `--navy:#0d1b2a`, `--cyan:#0891b2`, `--amber:#d97706`, `--purple:#7c3aed`) at three background variants (`.dark` navy, `.dark-green` deep-green, default white), reusable component classes (`.callout-*` colored left-border boxes, `.badge-*` pills, `.role-card`, `.artifact-card`, `.pillar-card`, `.event-card`, `.kanban-col`/`.kanban-card`, `.manifesto-val`, `.step-item`/`.step-num` numbered lists, `.check`/`.dot` bullet variants), and responsive typography sized in `px` at presentation scale (title 64px, body 32px, etc.) since the whole canvas is later scaled down as one unit. Navigation: a toolbar with Previous/Next chevron buttons and a "current / total" counter, an Expand/Collapse toggle, and a native browser-Fullscreen toggle (`requestFullscreen`/`exitFullscreen`, tracked via the `fullscreenchange` event). Keyboard support: ArrowRight/ArrowDown advances, ArrowLeft/ArrowUp goes back, Escape exits fullscreen. Below the slide, a row of pill/dot indicators (one per slide, current one wider) doubles as a scrubber — clicking any dot jumps directly to that slide.

## 4. Component & state architecture

A single default-export React component, `AgileScrumDeck()`. `SLIDES` is a module-level constant array of `{ classes, label, html, bg? }` objects — the deck's entire content lives as raw HTML strings rendered via `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` (this is a hand-authored, hardcoded slide set, not markdown-driven or CMS-backed). Component state: `current` (slide index), `expanded` (compact vs. expanded control layout), `fullscreen` (mirrors `document.fullscreenElement`). Three effects: (1) on mount, injects the `DECK_CSS` string into a `<style id="scrum-deck-styles">` tag in `document.head` if not already present, and removes it on unmount (a simple de-dup guard so multiple instances/remounts don't duplicate the stylesheet); (2) a `ResizeObserver` on the wrapping `<div>` recomputes and applies the scale transform whenever the container resizes; (3) a `keydown` listener for arrow-key navigation and Escape-to-exit-fullscreen, cleaned up on unmount. No Firestore reads/writes, no props beyond none (the component takes no arguments) — this is presentation-only with zero persisted state, unlike the quiz that immediately follows it in the course structure.

## 5. Rebuild notes

- The entire slide content is inline HTML strings inside one TypeScript file — there is no separate CMS or markdown source to reconcile; a rebuild is a matter of transcribing the 22 slides' text/structure faithfully (done in full above) rather than reverse-engineering data from elsewhere.
- The fixed 1920×1080 canvas + `ResizeObserver`-driven CSS-transform-scale pattern is reused verbatim by other decks in `src/components/slides/` in this codebase (e.g. other MBI802 decks use the same `.scale to fit` approach) — if reimplementing, this scaling mechanism is a good candidate to factor into a shared component rather than re-copying per deck, though the current codebase does not do so (each deck file duplicates its own `DECK_CSS` injection and scale-observer logic).
- The `#scrum-deck-styles` injected-stylesheet-with-cleanup pattern assumes only one instance of this specific deck is mounted at a time; if two instances were ever rendered simultaneously the unmount of either would remove the shared stylesheet out from under the other — not currently an issue given how it's used (single embed point in `CourseResources.tsx`), but worth flagging for a rebuild that might reuse the component elsewhere.
- No external links, images, or video embeds in this deck — everything is self-contained CSS/HTML/emoji, so there is no link-rot risk to revalidate, unlike several other lessons in this platform.
