Mini-Lecture — ER Diagram Fundamentals & Chen's Notation
  • What is an ER Diagram? Visual model of data, relationships, and constraints
  • Chen's notation symbols: Rectangle (entity), Ellipse (attribute), Diamond (relationship), Lines (participation)
  • Attribute types: simple, composite, multivalued (double ellipse), derived (dashed ellipse), key (underlined)
  • Relationships: binary (between 2 entities), ternary (between 3)
  • Cardinality: 1:1 (one-to-one), 1:N (one-to-many), M:N (many-to-many) — real-world examples for each

Instructor Notes
📝 Pedagogical Notes for the Instructor
  • Chen's vs. Crow's Foot: Students may encounter Crow's Foot notation online — briefly acknowledge it exists but keep focus on Chen's for this course.
  • Cardinality confusion: The most common error is placing cardinality labels on the wrong side of the relationship line. Use the "reading the relationship" technique: read from one entity to another and state the maximum.
  • M:N relationships: Students often add a foreign key directly — this is the mapping step (Class 6). In ER diagrams, M:N is represented with a diamond and lines only.
  • Gallery walk tip: Give each group a different coloured sticky note so authors know which feedback came from which peer group.
  • Tool recommendation: Encourage draw.io (free) or Lucidchart for digital ER diagrams — students will use these tools professionally.
