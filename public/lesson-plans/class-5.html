Mini-Lecture — Weak Entities & Identifying Relationships
  • What makes an entity "weak"? Cannot be uniquely identified without its owner entity
  • Chen's notation: double rectangle for weak entity, double diamond for identifying relationship
  • Partial key (discriminator): dashed underline — unique only within the context of the owner
  • Examples: OrderItem (depends on Order), RoomBooking (depends on Room), Dependent (depends on Employee)
  • Rule: a weak entity always has total participation in its identifying relationship
  • YooBees: Advanced ER Concepts deck — slides 1–5
11:10 – 11:30
20 min
Mini-Lecture — Composite, Multivalued & Derived Attributes
  • Composite attributes: decomposed into component sub-attributes (e.g., Address → {Street, City, State, PostCode})
  • When to decompose: if the system needs to query individual components separately
  • Multivalued attributes: double ellipse notation — an entity can have multiple values (e.g., PhoneNumbers, Skills, Languages)
  • Derived attributes: dashed ellipse — computed from other stored attributes (e.g., Age derived from DateOfBirth, TotalPrice derived from UnitPrice × Quantity)
  • YooBees: Advanced ER Concepts and ER Attribute Constraints decks — slides 6–11

12:00 – 12:20
20 min
Mini-Lecture — Participation Constraints
  • Total participation (double line): every entity in the set MUST participate in the relationship
  • Partial participation (single line): some entities MAY not participate
  • Example: every Employee MUST be assigned to a Department (total) — but not every Employee manages a Department (partial)
  • Min-Max notation: (min, max) notation as an alternative to total/partial representation
  • Structural constraints: combining cardinality ratio + participation constraint for full specification
  • YooBees: ER Attribute Constraints deck — slides 12–20

Case Study — Hospital Management System ER Diagram
  • Instructor builds a complete hospital ER diagram live on the board/screen

  • Students call out attributes for each entity as instructor draws (participatory building)
  • Pause points — class decides: Is this attribute composite? Should Prescription be a weak entity?

  • Identify M:N relationships: Doctor treats many Patients; Patient is treated by many Doctors

Instructor Notes
📝 Pedagogical Notes for the Instructor
  • Weak entity test: Teach students to ask "Can this entity exist without its owner?" — if no, it's weak.
  • Common error: Students confuse multivalued attributes with separate entities. If the values have their own attributes, it should be an entity. If they are simple values (e.g., phone numbers), use a multivalued attribute.
  • Participation constraint nuance: Total/partial applies per-entity-per-relationship — both sides must be assessed independently.
  • Hospital case study: Don't rush this. The participatory building approach is most effective when students feel ownership. Allow 2–3 minutes of genuine student discussion before resolving design decisions.
  • Homework check: YooBees platform tracks completion of deck exercises — review completion rate before Class 6.
