MBI800 Strategic Information Systems Planning

Group Project Tutorial

Strategic Information Systems Plan for a New Zealand or Australian Organisation

Dr Yasas Sri Wickramasinghe

1. Overview

In groups of 4 to 6, choose a real organisation based in New Zealand or Australia and prepare a strategic information systems (SIS) plan for its next three years. The plan must support the organisation's vision, mission and objectives, and must include:

  • a risk management framework
  • ethical and cultural considerations
  • at least one novel technology proposal (immersive technology such as AR, VR or MR, and/or AI or data science).

Each member is responsible for one part of the plan. You have five days to complete the work. Each group then presents its plan to the class.

2. Choosing an organisation

Choose an organisation that:

  • is based in New Zealand or Australia
  • publishes its vision, mission and strategic objectives
  • has a recent annual report, strategic plan or statement of intent available online
  • depends on information systems to serve its customers or run its operations.

Each group must choose a different organisation. Do not choose the organisations already covered in lectures (Inland Revenue, Xero, Canva, Airbnb, Netflix, Alibaba, STC).

Section 1: Executive summary

Half a page. The organisation, the main IS gaps, the recommended initiatives, the key risks and the first steps.

Section 2: Organisation and strategic direction

  • Short profile: sector, size, ownership, customers, competitors.
  • Vision, mission, values and strategic objectives (quoted and cited).
  • SWOT analysis and Business Model Canvas (Lectures 4 and 5).

Section 3: Current information systems

  • Main systems and digital channels the organisation uses.
  • Iceberg Model analysis of one recurring problem (Lecture 2).
  • Six process dimensions: how the organisation plans its IS (Lecture 3).
  • Gap analysis: objectives compared with what current systems support.

Section 4: IS strategy and initiatives

  • Three to five IS strategic themes linked to the organisation's objectives.
  • Four to six initiatives, each checked with the four feasibility tests: operational, technical, economic and schedule (Lecture 4).
  • Priority of each initiative, with reasons.

Section 5: Risk management

  • Choose a framework (NIST RMF, ISO/IEC 27005, COBIT, OCTAVE or FAIR) and explain why it suits this organisation (Lecture 6).
  • Apply the steps of the framework to the plan.
  • Risk register with at least eight risks. Columns: risk, likelihood (1 to 5), impact (1 to 5), rating, controls, owner.
  • Controls for the top three risks, including security, backup and recovery (Lecture 10).

Section 6: Ethical and cultural considerations

  • Privacy law: Privacy Act 2020 (NZ) or Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles.
  • Indigenous data sovereignty: Te Tiriti o Waitangi and the Te Mana Raraunga principles (NZ), or the Maiam nayri Wingara principles (Australia). The CARE principles apply in both.
  • Consent, consultation with affected communities, and accessibility.
  • Fairness, transparency and human oversight where AI or automated decisions are used.
  • A stakeholder table: stakeholder, impact, concern, response.

Section 7: Novel technology proposal

  • One or two proposals using immersive technology (AR, VR or MR) and/or AI or data science (Lecture 9).
  • The objective it supports and the problem it solves.
  • Users, data required and technology needed.
  • Four feasibility tests and an Idea Canvas (Lectures 4 and 5).
  • A pilot plan: scope, duration and success measures.

Section 8: Implementation roadmap

A timeline over three years (0 to 12, 12 to 24 and 24 to 36 months) showing each initiative, who is responsible, and how progress will be measured.

Section 9: Conclusion and references

A short conclusion and a reference list in APA 7. Put large tables and canvases in appendices.

5. Presentation

  • Each team has 15 minutes.
  • Every student must present their own part.
  • Record the presentation with audio (for example in Microsoft Teams, Zoom or PowerPoint).

6. Peer rating

During the presentations, each student completes one peer rating sheet. Rate every group except your own on a scale of 1 to 5 for each criterion, and write one strength and one improvement for each group.
