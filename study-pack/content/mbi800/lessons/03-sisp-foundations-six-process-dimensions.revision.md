## Formal definition (Segars, Grover & Teng, 1998)

SISP = formal process at:

- **Scope** — broad
- **Perspective** — upper management
- **Time frame** — long range
- **Level of abstraction** — conceptual

Missing any one → not really SISP.

## Core agenda (3 jobs)

1. **Support and influence** — identify value-adding systems, drive strategic direction
2. **Technological integration** — coordinate disparate tech into shared architecture
3. **Implementation strategy** — macro-level blueprints, not project specs

## The Six Process Dimensions

| Dimension | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Comprehensiveness | How thorough — alternatives, data, risk weighing, contingencies? |
| Formalization | Written procedures / explicit policy vs ad-hoc? |
| Focus | Innovative orientation vs integrative (control) orientation? |
| Flow | Top-down (centralised) vs bottom-up (functional mgmt initiates)? |
| Participation | Narrow (one team) vs broad (many functional areas)? |
| Consistency | Infrequent/ad-hoc vs continuous, iterative? |

::: tip
Participation ≠ surveying people after the plan is done. It's about power/legitimacy — informed vs co-designing.
:::

::: tip
Consistency ≠ meeting frequency alone. Every IS decision must trace to a strategic theme.
:::

## Rational Adaptation proposition

Best-performing processes balance:

- **Rational Tendencies:** high comprehensiveness, strong formalization, integrative/control focus, top-down flow
- **Adaptive Tendencies:** broad participation, high consistency

::: warning
Rational without Adaptive = thorough plan, stale within a year. Adaptive without Rational = constant activity, no discipline. Neither alone works.
:::

## Diagnostic pattern (exam use)

Rate a described process on all six dimensions → classify Rational vs Adaptive tendencies present/missing → name the weakest dimension → state the specific risk that weakness creates.
