## Business model

A **business model** = how an idea creates value.

**Common revenue patterns:** product/service sales · subscription · ad revenue · commission · freemium.

## The Business Model Canvas (9 blocks)

| Left / cost side | Centre | Right / revenue side |
|---|---|---|
| Key Partners | **Value Proposition** | Customer Relationships |
| Key Activities | | Channels |
| Key Resources | | Customer Segments |
| **Cost Structure** (bottom-left) | | **Revenue Streams** (bottom-right) |

- Key Partners — mentors, lawyers, distributors, suppliers?
- Key Activities — what will you spend most time doing?
- Key Resources — manufacturing, IP, HR costs?
- Customer Relationships — what interaction do customers expect?
- Channels — how do customers find you / get the product?
- Customer Segments — who pays? non-paying users? how big?
- Cost Structure — time + money required
- Revenue Streams — how do you make $? what non-monetary value?

## The Idea Canvas (adapted, non-commercial)

Same 9 blocks, relabelled:

| Business Model term | Idea Canvas term |
|---|---|
| Value Proposition | Your Idea (project / lesson / thesis / hypothesis) |
| Customer Segments | Audience Segments |
| Customer Relationships | Audience Relationships |
| Key Partners | Teachers, peers, parents, subject experts |
| Cost Structure | Cost of your idea (time, collaborators, materials) |
| Revenue Streams | Value of your idea — **social · academic · monetary** |

::: tip
The Idea Canvas explicitly separates social/academic value from monetary value — an idea can be "valuable" with zero revenue.
:::

## Why it matters for SISP

A one-page canvas tests whether value proposition, audience and cost cohere — **before** the heavier machinery of a formal business case (Ch.4) or SISP evaluation (Ch.3) is engaged.
