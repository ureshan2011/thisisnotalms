## Case 1 — Saudi Telecommunications Company (STC)

Structured 5-year SISP plan, formal IS assessment/vision, cross-departmental (managers/analysts/programmers/consultants).

Strategic focus: broadband leadership · customer service · MENA expansion · human capital · financial performance · brand.

Survey (75 staff): strong mgmt support, "right people" hired, clear IS strategy, adaptable, open culture, formal known planning process.

**Impact:** proactive not reactive; IS = competitive advantage.

## Case 2 — Large organisations, Ghana

Framework: **Segars & Grover's 4 dimensions** + Resource-Based Theory.

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Alignment | IS goals track changing strategy; opportunities pursued (cloud, banking) |
| Analysis | SWOT/internal assessment; continuous digitisation |
| Cooperation | Cross-dept workshops; avoids redundant development |
| Capabilities | Anticipate crises (SWOT/PESTLE/risk logs); capacity-building |
| **Resource Availability** | Critical **moderator** — well-resourced orgs succeed more |

::: tip
All 4 dimensions can look good on paper and still fail without adequate resources — a policy-level issue, not just a management one.
:::

## Case 3 — NZ Inland Revenue Business Transformation

**Not** framed as "just a tech upgrade" — full organisational transformation.

- Digital self-service system
- Process review (redesigned, not just digitised)
- Engaged customers, businesses, tax professionals

**Success factors:** customer-centric design · comprehensive planning · adaptability.

**Impact:** simpler processes, fewer errors, positive user feedback.

## Common thread across all 3

1. Explicit **IS-strategy alignment**
2. **Cross-functional** engagement (not IT-only)
3. **Formal planning discipline**
4. **Adequate resourcing** as precondition

::: warning
None credit success to a specific technology/vendor choice. SISP failures are rarely *technology* failures.
:::
