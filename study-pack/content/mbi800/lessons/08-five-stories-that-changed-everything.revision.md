## The pattern

In each story, the durable advantage was an **information system**, not the product category.

| Company | Core IS concept | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Airbnb** (2007) | Trust infra + two-sided marketplace: 2-way reviews, escrow payment (24h post-check-in) | Trust can be engineered |
| **Netflix** (1997) | Data flywheel: viewing data → recommendations → retention → more subscribers | Disrupt your own profitable line first |
| **Xero** (2006) | Single source of truth: one cloud ledger + 1,000+ app API ecosystem | Empower the middleman (accountants), don't bypass |
| **Canva** (2007) | Product-led growth, 2-sided marketplace, hides complexity behind templates | Removing a barrier can reveal a hidden market |
| **Alibaba** (1999) | Integrated ecosystem: Alipay (payments/escrow) + Cainiao (logistics) + Alibaba Cloud | Build the unglamorous trust infrastructure nobody else will |

## Key facts

- Airbnb: owns **zero** inventory; 8M+ listings, 220+ countries, ~$75B market cap.
- Netflix: DVD/streaming split 2011 lost 800k subscribers, stock -75% — kept the bet anyway. Blockbuster declined to buy Netflix for $50M (2000); bankrupt 2010.
- Xero: bank feeds auto-populate the ledger; switching costs compound with every connection wired in.
- Canva: "Made with Canva" export = viral loop; used inside 95% of Fortune 500, adopted bottom-up not sold top-down.
- Alibaba: Alipay escrow solved what eBay's commission marketplace couldn't — beat eBay in China without a "better marketplace."

## Risks named per company

- Airbnb — depends entirely on hosts it doesn't employ (asset-light = limited control)
- Netflix — willingness to cannibalise its own revenue
- Xero — lock-in via switching costs (double-edged)
- Canva — AI may commoditise the design skill it made accessible
- Alibaba — private company operating de facto national economic infrastructure

::: tip
Two stories reversed "obvious" strategy: Netflix disrupted itself; Xero empowered rather than bypassed the middleman.
:::
